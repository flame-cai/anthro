# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

We want `anthro` to be a welcoming, respectful, and constructive open-source project.

Participants are expected to contribute in good faith and to treat other contributors, users, maintainers, researchers, and members of the wider public-health and open-source communities with respect.

## Expected behavior

Examples of behavior that contribute to a positive environment include:

- Being respectful and professional.
- Assuming good intent while addressing disagreements constructively.
- Providing useful, technically grounded feedback.
- Respecting different backgrounds, experiences, and areas of expertise.
- Focusing criticism on ideas, implementations, documentation, or evidence rather than individuals.
- Being open to correction.
- Giving appropriate credit to previous work and external sources.
- Respecting the provenance and licensing requirements of third-party material.
- Taking care when discussing health, nutrition, anthropometry, or other sensitive applications of the project.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following behavior is not acceptable:

- Harassment, discrimination, intimidation, or personal attacks.
- Threats or deliberately abusive behavior.
- Trolling or sustained disruptive behavior.
- Publishing another person's private information without permission.
- Deliberately misleading others about project authorship, affiliation, certification, or endorsement.
- Claiming that this project is official WHO software or that it has been reviewed, certified, verified, or endorsed by WHO when that is not the case.
- Deliberately misrepresenting the provenance or licensing of data or other third-party material.
- Other conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional open-source community.

## Technical disagreements

Technical disagreement is expected and encouraged when handled constructively.

When discussing methodology, numerical results, reference data, or compatibility with another implementation, contributors should prefer:

- reproducible examples;
- test cases;
- source references;
- quantitative comparisons; and
- clear descriptions of assumptions and limitations.

## Maintainer responsibilities

Maintainers are responsible for:

- applying this Code of Conduct consistently;
- keeping project discussions reasonably constructive;
- addressing reported problems in good faith;
- protecting private information where appropriate; and
- documenting important project decisions when useful to the community.

## Enforcement

Concerns about unacceptable behavior may be reported privately to the project maintainer through an appropriate private communication channel.

Reports should include enough information to understand the concern and, where possible, relevant links or examples.

The maintainer may take any action considered appropriate, including:

- requesting a change in behavior;
- removing inappropriate content;
- temporarily restricting participation; or
- permanently restricting participation.

Retaliation against a person who makes a good-faith report is not acceptable.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to project repositories, issue discussions, pull requests, documentation discussions, and other spaces used specifically for the `anthro` project.
